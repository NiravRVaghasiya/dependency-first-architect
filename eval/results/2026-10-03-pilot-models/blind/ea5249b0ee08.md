# Multi-Tenant SaaS Billing System Architecture

I'm assuming you're building a platform where many customer companies (tenants) bill their own end customers, like a Stripe Billing or Chargebee-style system. If you only need to bill your own tenants, you can drop the tenant-of-tenant layer. That removes a lot of complexity.

## 1. Requirements

**Functional**
- Product catalog with plans, prices, and add-ons
- Subscriptions, including trials, upgrades and downgrades, proration, pauses, and cancellations
- Usage-based billing (metered, tiered, volume, and committed-use pricing)
- Invoicing, credit notes, and discounts or coupons
- Payment collection, retries (dunning), and refunds
- Tax calculation
- Revenue recognition exports
- Customer portal and webhooks

**Non-functional**
- **Correctness over availability for money.** Billing must never double-charge or lose usage.
- Strict tenant isolation
- Auditability: every financial change is traceable and immutable
- Horizontal scale for usage ingestion (10k+ events/sec per large tenant)
- Idempotent APIs everywhere

## 2. High-Level Architecture

```
                ┌───────────────────────────────┐
  Clients ─────▶│ API Gateway (authN, rate limit│
  (API/Portal)  │ tenant resolution, idempotency)│
                └──────────────┬────────────────┘
        ┌───────────┬──────────┼───────────┬────────────┐
        ▼           ▼          ▼           ▼            ▼
   Catalog     Subscription  Usage      Invoicing    Payments
   Service     Service       Metering   Service      Service
        │           │          │           │            │
        └───────────┴────┬─────┴───────────┴────────────┘
                         ▼
              Event Bus (Kafka / Redpanda)
       ┌─────────┬───────┼─────────┬──────────┐
       ▼         ▼       ▼         ▼          ▼
    Ledger    Tax      Dunning  Notifications  Webhooks
    Service   Adapter  Engine   (email/in-app) Dispatcher
       │
       ▼
   Data Warehouse / Rev-Rec / Reporting
```

Start as a **modular monolith** with these as internal modules, and split out only Usage Metering and Payments first. Those two have the most different scaling and risk profiles. A billing team of under 15 people will struggle with 10 microservices.

## 3. Multi-Tenancy Strategy

| Model | Use when |
|---|---|
| **Shared DB, shared schema + `tenant_id` + Postgres Row-Level Security** (default) | Most tenants; cheapest and simplest to operate |
| Schema-per-tenant | Mid-tier tenants needing stronger logical separation |
| Database-per-tenant | Enterprise or regulated tenants (data residency, dedicated encryption keys) |

**Recommendation:** Use a pooled model with RLS by default and a "cell" architecture for scale and isolation. Each cell is a full stack with its own DB cluster, and tenants are pinned to a cell by a routing table. Enterprise tenants can get a dedicated cell.

**Enforcement layers**
1. The gateway resolves the tenant from the API key or JWT claim. It is never taken from the request body.
2. The app sets `SET LOCAL app.tenant_id = ...` per transaction.
3. RLS policies on every table: `USING (tenant_id = current_setting('app.tenant_id')::uuid)`.
4. All composite indexes and primary keys lead with `tenant_id`.
5. Kafka messages carry `tenant_id` as the partition key, which preserves per-tenant ordering.
6. Per-tenant rate limits and quotas prevent noisy neighbors.
7. Per-tenant encryption keys (envelope encryption via KMS) for sensitive fields.

## 4. Core Domain Model

```
Tenant ─< Customer ─< Subscription ─< SubscriptionItem >─ Price >─ Product
                │            │
                │            └─< UsageRecord (via Meter)
                ├─< PaymentMethod
                └─< Invoice ─< InvoiceLineItem
                       ├─< Payment ─< Refund
                       └─< CreditNote
LedgerAccount ─< LedgerEntry   (double-entry, append-only)
```

**Key design decisions**
- **Prices are immutable.** Changing a price creates a new version, and subscriptions reference a specific price ID. This avoids retroactive billing bugs.
- **Money** is stored as integer minor units plus an ISO 4217 currency code. Never use floats.
- **Invoices are immutable once finalized.** Corrections go through credit notes or new invoices.
- **Time:** store all timestamps in UTC and store the billing anchor and timezone explicitly per subscription.

## 5. Key Subsystems

### 5.1 Subscription Lifecycle
State machine: `trialing → active → past_due → (unpaid | canceled)`, plus `paused` and `incomplete`.
- A scheduler (durable timers through Temporal or a DB-backed job queue) triggers renewals, trial ends, and scheduled changes.
- **Proration:** compute by the second or day, and generate credit and charge line items on the next invoice (or immediately, as a tenant option).
- Plan changes are stored as **scheduled subscription phases**, which makes upgrades, downgrades, and future-dated changes uniform.

### 5.2 Usage Metering Pipeline
```
SDK/API → Ingest API (dedupe by idempotency key) → Kafka
       → Stream aggregator (Flink/ksqlDB or custom consumers)
       → Aggregates in ClickHouse / Timescale (per meter, customer, window)
       → Billing-period close snapshots usage → Invoicing
```
- Each event has `tenant_id, customer_id, meter_id, timestamp, quantity, idempotency_key`.
- Handle **late events** with a grace window before period close. After close, late usage rolls into the next invoice or is adjusted explicitly.
- Keep raw events in object storage (S3/Parquet) so any invoice can be reproduced.
- At invoice time, freeze the usage snapshot and store its hash on the invoice.

### 5.3 Rating and Invoicing
- The **rating engine** is a pure, deterministic function: `(price model, quantity, context) → line items`. It supports flat, per-unit, tiered, volume, package, and percentage pricing.
- **Invoice run:** at period end, create a draft, add line items (recurring, usage, proration, discounts), calculate tax, finalize (assign a gapless sequential number per tenant, as some jurisdictions require), and emit `invoice.finalized`.
- A grace period on drafts (for example 1 hour) lets tenants review or adjust.
- Generate PDFs asynchronously and store them in object storage.

### 5.4 Payments
- Use a **PSP abstraction** (Stripe, Adyen, Braintree) behind an internal interface. Never store card data. Use tokenization to stay at PCI SAQ-A.
- Payments follow a state machine: `pending → processing → succeeded | failed | requires_action`.
- **Idempotency keys** are derived from `invoice_id + attempt_number` on every PSP call.
- Make PSP webhooks the source of truth, and reconcile against PSP settlement reports daily to catch missed webhooks.
- **Tenant payouts:** if you process money for tenants, you need a marketplace model such as Stripe Connect. That brings KYC, split payments, and payout scheduling.

### 5.5 Dunning
A configurable retry schedule per tenant (for example days 1, 3, 5, 7). It uses smart retries based on the decline code (soft and hard declines differ), sends emails, and ends with a terminal action (cancel or mark unpaid).

### 5.6 Double-Entry Ledger
Every money movement posts balanced entries to an append-only ledger:
- Invoice finalized: Dr Accounts Receivable, Cr Revenue (or Deferred Revenue) and Cr Tax Payable
- Payment: Dr Cash/PSP Clearing, Cr Accounts Receivable
- Refund and credit note: reverse entries

This gives you auditability, customer balance and credits, and clean revenue recognition (ASC 606/IFRS 15 schedules). Enforce `SUM(debits) = SUM(credits)` per transaction with a DB constraint or check.

### 5.7 Tax
Integrate Avalara, TaxJar, or Stripe Tax behind an adapter. Store the tax determination (jurisdiction, rate, and the request and response) on the invoice. Support VAT ID validation and reverse charge.

### 5.8 Multi-currency
Each price is defined in explicit currencies (no implicit conversion). The invoice currency is fixed at creation. FX rates are snapshotted only for reporting.

## 6. Data Storage

| Data | Store |
|---|---|
| Transactional (catalog, subs, invoices, ledger) | PostgreSQL (partitioned by `tenant_id` hash or time) |
| Usage events, aggregates | Kafka → ClickHouse; raw to S3 |
| Idempotency keys, rate limits, caches | Redis |
| Documents (PDFs, exports) | S3 with per-tenant prefix and KMS |
| Analytics and rev-rec | Warehouse (Snowflake/BigQuery) via CDC |
| Audit log | Append-only table, mirrored to WORM storage |

Use the **transactional outbox pattern** to publish events reliably. Write the state change and the outbox row in one transaction, then have a relay (Debezium) publish to Kafka.

## 7. API Design

- REST, with webhooks for async events. Example resources: `/v1/customers`, `/v1/subscriptions`, `/v1/invoices`, `/v1/usage-events`.
- The `Idempotency-Key` header is required on all POSTs. Store the key with a request hash and the response for 24h or more.
- Cursor pagination and versioned APIs (date-based versions, as Stripe does).
- **Webhooks:** HMAC-signed, retried with exponential backoff, with a replay UI and per-endpoint delivery logs.
- Scoped API keys (read-only, restricted) and OAuth for integrations.

## 8. Security and Compliance

- **AuthN/Z:** OIDC for the dashboard, API keys for machines, and RBAC per tenant (owner, finance, developer, read-only).
- **PCI:** Use PSP-hosted fields or tokens so card data never touches your systems.
- **Encryption:** TLS everywhere, with KMS-managed keys and field-level encryption for PII and tax IDs.
- **GDPR:** Erasure conflicts with financial retention rules. Pseudonymize PII while retaining invoice records, and document the legal basis.
- **SOC 2:** Audit logging, change management, and least-privilege access. Engineers get no direct prod DB access without break-glass.
- **Data residency:** Use cell placement by region.

## 9. Reliability and Correctness

- **Idempotency** at every boundary: API, consumers (dedupe by event ID), and PSP calls.
- **Sagas or workflow engine (Temporal)** for multi-step processes such as invoice run → tax → finalize → charge → notify. This gives durable retries and visibility.
- **Reconciliation jobs (daily):**
  - Ledger balances to zero.
  - Invoices paid match PSP settlements.
  - Usage totals match invoiced quantities.
- **Backfill and replay:** because events are immutable and rating is deterministic, you can re-rate a period in a sandbox and diff it against the real invoice.
- **Billing-run sharding:** spread by `tenant_id` and jitter the start times. Avoid everyone being billed at 00:00 UTC on the 1st.
- **DR:** Multi-AZ Postgres with PITR, a cross-region replica, RPO ≤ 1 min, and RTO ≤ 30 min for the payments path.

## 10. Observability

- Trace IDs follow a request through events, PSP calls, and webhooks.
- Business metrics: invoice success rate, payment failure rate by decline code, MRR/ARR, usage lag, dunning recovery rate, and ledger imbalance (which should always be zero and should page on-call).
- Per-tenant dashboards and SLOs: ingest latency p99, invoice generation completion within X hours of period end.

## 11. Suggested Tech Stack

- **Language:** Go, Kotlin/Java, or TypeScript (NestJS). Choose by team skill; the design doesn't depend on it.
- **DB:** PostgreSQL. **Streaming:** Kafka or Redpanda. **Analytics:** ClickHouse. **Workflows:** Temporal. **Cache:** Redis.
- **Infra:** Kubernetes, Terraform, and cells per region.
- **Build vs. buy:** If billing isn't your core product, use Stripe Billing, Orb, Metronome, or Lago and build only the integration layer. A custom build costs years of edge cases (tax, proration, dunning).

## 12. Phased Rollout

1. **MVP:** Catalog, flat-rate subscriptions, invoices, one PSP, basic ledger, webhooks, pooled tenancy with RLS.
2. **Phase 2:** Usage metering, tiered pricing, proration, dunning, tax integration, customer portal.
3. **Phase 3:** Multi-currency, revenue recognition, credit notes and balances, enterprise contracts (commitments, custom invoicing), cell-based isolation.
4. **Phase 4:** Marketplace payouts, data residency, a quoting and CPQ integration, and advanced analytics.

## 13. Main Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Double charging | Idempotency keys, a payment state machine, and reconciliation |
| Cross-tenant data leak | RLS, automated tenant-isolation tests in CI, and pen tests |
| Usage data loss or duplication | Dedupe keys, a durable log, and raw event retention |
| Pricing-model sprawl | A declarative price schema and a single rating engine |
| Tax and compliance errors | Use a provider and store determinations |
| Billing-day thundering herd | Sharding, jitter, and autoscaled workers |

## Questions That Would Change the Design

1. Are you billing your own tenants, or enabling tenants to bill their customers (and moving money for them)?
2. What is the usage volume, and is pricing usage-based or seat-based?
3. Which regions and currencies, and what tax exposure?
4. Do enterprise customers need custom contracts or invoicing (net-30, PO numbers)?
5. What is the team size, and is build vs. buy still open?
