# BUILD PLAN: Customer-support RAG chatbot over help-center docs

**Working assumption (sets the phase order):** in v1 the bot only answers questions from public help-center articles and hands off to a human through your existing ticketing system. It does not look up accounts or orders, and it does not issue refunds or change anything. If v1 must do any of those, the plan changes: an identity and authorization phase and a tool allow-list with human approval for writes come before any retrieval work reaches customers, and the trust boundary becomes an R3 gate. Tell me if that applies and I'll re-plan.

---

## 1. Classification and constraints
- **What:** a chat widget on the help center. It answers customer questions from published articles, cites the source article, says so when it doesn't know, and hands off to a human agent.
- **Type:** an AI system plus a software service. The service is new, but it plugs into two existing systems: the help-center platform (system of record for content) and the ticketing system (system of record for escalations). The existing search and contact form stay as the fallback. **Not a small build** because outside customers use it.
- **Dominant constraint:** correctness (answers must be grounded in the docs).
- **Worst failure:** the bot confidently states a wrong or made-up policy (refund, billing, security, cancellation) and the customer acts on it. Companies have been held liable for what their bot said. The second-worst failure is the bot showing internal or unpublished articles to the public.

**Missing inputs** (most plan-changing first):
1. **Account-aware answers or actions in v1?** I assumed no (see top).
2. **Help-center platform and its API.** Is it Zendesk Guide, Intercom, Salesforce Knowledge, Confluence, or a static site? Does it have publish/unpublish webhooks? Does it have internal-only or segment-restricted articles? I assumed restricted articles exist, so v1 indexes public articles only.
3. **Customer regions and languages.** EU customers bring GDPR and possibly data-residency requirements, which constrain the LLM vendor (V3). I assumed v1 is English only.
4. **Ticketing system for handoff** and its API access.
5. **Volume, human cost per ticket, SLO.** Needed for the UNKNOWN rows above.
6. **Real questions for the eval set.** These come from historical tickets and help-center search logs, which contain personal data, so a data-access approval is needed.
7. **Named owners:** support lead (answer quality sign-off), docs owner (content gaps), security lead (red team), privacy/legal (vendor terms, disclaimer, topics the bot may answer).

## 3. Tradeoff gates (resolved up front)

| Decision | Reversibility | Default (chosen now) | Assumption | Validated by | Flip condition |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Consistency vs availability (index vs help center) | R2: citing a retracted policy hurts customers, but the index can be rebuilt | Eventually consistent: webhook-driven incremental sync, a nightly full reconcile, and unpublish handled as a priority delete | The platform has publish/unpublish webhooks | V1 (freshness ≤ 15 min) | No webhooks: poll every 5 min plus a check at answer time that each cited article is still published |
| Build vs buy | R2: weeks of build lost if we switch; buying puts transcripts and tuning on the vendor's side | Build a thin service on a hosted LLM API | No native bot from the help-center vendor meets V5 at lower total cost | Phase 0 desk comparison of vendor per-resolution pricing; in Phase 3, run the vendor bot (if one exists) on the V5 eval set | The vendor bot passes V5 and costs per resolution ≤ our V6 figure: buy it, and keep the eval set and guardrail tests as acceptance tests |
| Data-privacy boundary | R3: personal data sent to a vendor, or trained on, cannot be recalled | Hosted API under a DPA (no training, zero or minimal retention, region-pinned if required). Presidio redacts card numbers, government IDs, and similar before the LLM call and before logging. Handoff contact details go in a form field and never reach the LLM. Transcripts live in our Postgres with a TTL | Vendor terms and region meet our privacy commitments | V3 | V3 fails, or residency can't be met: switch to a regional endpoint or vendor, or self-host (re-plan) |
| Corpus boundary (what is indexed) | R3: showing internal articles to the public cannot be undone | Index only published, publicly visible articles, filtered at ingestion (not at query time) | Article visibility is exposed by the API | V1 | Segment-restricted articles are needed: add per-user access filtering at retrieval, with identity (new R3 gate) |
| Answer scope and abstention | R3: the company may be held to what the bot says | Answer only from retrieved passages, with validated citations. Abstain and offer handoff when the docs don't cover the question. Sensitive topics (refund or credit commitments, legal, security incidents, account-specific questions) get a fixed template plus handoff | Grounded generation can meet the V5 bar | V5 | V5 fails: fall back to search with extracted snippets (no free-text generation) |
| Prompt+RAG vs fine-tune | R2 for fine-tuning; R3 if trained on transcripts | Prompt+RAG | Docs change often, citations need retrieval, and fine-tuning can't track edits (cited basis) | V4/V5 | V4 passes but V5 fails on tone or format: fine-tune on curated, non-personal examples only |
| Hosted API vs self-host | R2: the provider seam exists from Phase 0 | Hosted API behind a `ModelClient` interface, with a fallback model | Hosted terms and cost are acceptable | V3, V6 | V3 fails on residency, or V6 fails at volume |

**R1 defaults:** monolith vs services → one deployable with a separate ingestion worker process; sync vs async → answers streamed synchronously over SSE, ingestion async from a queue table; vector store → pgvector in managed Postgres (corpus assumed under 10k articles); embedding model → pinned inside the index version (re-embedding takes hours at this size); search → hybrid (Postgres full-text + vector), with a reranker only if V4 needs it; feature flags → Unleash or LaunchDarkly; eval harness → promptfoo or pytest in CI.
**N/A:** migration strategy and cutover — nothing is being replaced. The contact form and search stay as the permanent fallback.

**Inherited constraints:** the help center is the system of record for content, and the bot never edits it (changing that would mean a new content workflow). The ticketing system is the system of record for escalations.

## 4. Walking skeleton (Phase 0)
- **Request:** an allow-listed support agent, signed in with company SSO, asks a real question ("How do I reset my two-factor authentication?") in the widget on the production help center. The widget appears only when the feature flag is on. The agent gets a streamed answer citing the source article, plus a "Talk to a human" link to the existing contact form.
- **Tiers:** widget → chat API (SSO-checked) → retrieval over a pgvector index built by one real ingestion run of public articles through the help-center API → hosted LLM through `ModelClient` → answer rendered with citations → transcript and trace written to Postgres.
- **Already in place on day zero:**
  - The system prompt is kept apart from user text and retrieved text. Retrieved passages go in a delimited data block and are treated as data, never as instructions.
  - The renderer allows links only to the help-center domain and renders no images or raw HTML.
  - Hard token caps apply per turn and per session, and the provider-side spend limit is set on the key.
- **Deploy / log / monitor / rollback:** container built in CI and deployed by IaC (Terraform) to production. OpenTelemetry carries one trace ID from widget to LLM. Metrics cover tokens, cost, latency, and error rate, with alerts on error rate and spend. Rollback means redeploying the previous image digest, with the flag off as the kill switch.
- **Live-path baseline:** record the current help-center → contact-form rate, ticket volume per topic, and CSAT as BASELINE values for V6. Confirm the help-center and ticketing APIs (turn the ASSUMED rows to CONFIRMED). Start the organizational items: DPA, ticket-data access, named owners, cost-per-ticket input.
- **Who can reach it:** allow-listed staff only. No customer traffic and no customer data.
- **Exit check (V0):** see §6.

## 5. Phases

**Phase 1 — Defenses and budgets**
- Unlocks: any exposure path. Phases 2–3 can be built against a guarded system.
- Depends on: V0.
- Tasks:
  1. Corpus-boundary filter at ingestion, plus a diff of indexed IDs against the public listing on every sync.
  2. Unpublish handled as a priority delete.
  3. Injection suite in CI covering user turns and a planted test article in a staging index.
  4. Presidio redaction before the LLM call and before logs.
  5. Sensitive-topic classifier routing to the handoff template.
  6. Caps on tokens per turn and turns per session. A daily spend kill switch that turns the flag off automatically. Timeout leading to the degraded "matching articles" response. Circuit breaker on the LLM.
  7. Human-in-the-loop rule: the model has no tools. Ticket creation runs only from the user's confirmed button click in the UI handler, never from model output.
  8. Transcripts classified as customer personal data, with restricted access and a TTL.
- Rollback: guardrail config is versioned; revert it with a redeploy.
- Exit check: V1 and V2 pass. A synthetic runaway session is cut off at the cap and emits a cost metric.

**Phase 2 — Retrieval**
- Unlocks: answer tuning.
- Depends on: Phase 1 and the eval-data access approval.
- Tasks:
  1. Chunk schema: article section with heading path, plus metadata (article ID, locale, product, updated_at, visibility).
  2. Index version = embedding model + chunker version + corpus snapshot.
  3. Hybrid search.
  4. Labeled eval set of at least 200 PII-scrubbed real questions with their source passages, plus at least 100 questions the docs don't answer.
  5. Content-gap report to the docs owner.
- Rollback: blue/green index swap, keeping the previous index version.
- Exit check: V4 passes.

**Phase 3 — Model access, memory, orchestration (answer generation)**
- Unlocks: a customer canary.
- Depends on: V4.
- Tasks:
  1. One fixed chain: rewrite the follow-up into a standalone query, retrieve, generate structured output (answer, citations[], abstain flag), then validate the citations. Each citation must be a passage that was actually retrieved and is still published. Failing validation means abstain.
  2. Conversation memory within the session only (last N turns).
  3. Fallback model selectable by config.
  4. Retries with backoff.
  5. Versioned prompt registry.
  6. Build-vs-buy bake-off on the same eval set.
- Rollback: pin the previous prompt + model + index version.
- Exit check: V5 passes on the primary and the fallback model. Operations stay within the complexity budget.

**Phase 4 — Handoff and customer canary**
- Unlocks: measuring real resolution and cost.
- Depends on: V1, V2, V3, V5, and the throughput input.
- Tasks:
  1. Ticketing handoff: the user confirms a ticket that carries the redacted transcript, with a scoped API token.
  2. Brief the support agents.
  3. Load test at 2× the projected canary peak.
  4. Per-IP and per-session rate limits, with a CAPTCHA when abuse is detected.
  5. Canary to 1%, then 5%, of anonymous English help-center visitors, assigned deterministically by cohort. The contact form stays visible.
  6. Auto-disable the flag on a breach of the error, spend, or complaint threshold.
- Rollback: flag off, which drops customers back to the existing path at no cost.
- Exit check: V6 passes.

**Phase 5 — Widen and feedback**
- Unlocks: general availability.
- Depends on: V6 and the SLO input.
- Tasks:
  1. Widen to 25%, 50%, then 100%. Each step is held until its dashboard check passes against the Phase 0 BASELINE.
  2. Capture thumbs up/down and agent-tagged "bot was wrong" on handed-off tickets.
  3. Weekly human review feeds corrections into the eval set and doc fixes. Nothing is applied to prompts automatically.
  4. SLIs and burn-rate alerts.
  5. Quarterly red-team.
- Rollback: step back down the canary ladder, or turn the flag off.
- Exit check: the SLO is met for 30 days. Each feedback-driven change improves the eval with no regressions.

## 10. Deliberately deferred
- **Account-aware answers and actions (order status, refunds):** pulled forward if the business needs them. Requires identity, an authorization design, a tool allow-list, and approval gates for writes.
- **Segment-restricted or logged-in-only articles:** pulled forward if more than about 10% of handed-off questions are covered only by restricted articles. Requires per-user access filtering (new R3 gate).
- **Languages other than English:** pulled forward if the canary shows meaningful non-English demand. Needs locale eval sets per language.
- **Memory across sessions:** pulled forward if users need to resume conversations. Needs a privacy review of what gets written.
- **Cheaper-model routing:** pulled forward if V6 fails on cost or volume grows enough to matter.
- **Fine-tuning:** pulled forward by the flip condition in §3.
- **Self-hosting:** pulled forward if V3 fails on residency or V6 fails at volume.
- **Internal agent-assist copilot on the same retrieval:** pulled forward if V5 fails for customer-facing use. It is lower risk with a human in the loop.
- **Email and voice channels:** pulled forward after the chat SLO holds for 30 days.
