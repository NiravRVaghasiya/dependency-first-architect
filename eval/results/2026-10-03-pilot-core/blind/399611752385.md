# Plan: Moving the Patient Scheduling System to the Cloud Without Downtime

I don't have details about your system, so this plan uses common assumptions. The questions at the end will let me fit it to your setup.

**The key point:** A scheduling system needs a single source of truth to prevent double-booking. That rules out simply running two live copies side by side. The safe approach has three parts:

- **Copy the data continuously.** Change data capture (CDC) keeps the cloud database in step with the on-prem one.
- **Move read-only traffic first.**
- **Switch the database that accepts bookings in one step.** Most systems need a pause in new bookings of seconds to a few minutes. Users can still view schedules during the pause, so clinics see no outage.

Truly zero write pause is only possible if the application was built for it. Plan for near-zero, and still schedule the switch for your quietest period.

---

## Phase 0: Discovery (2–4 weeks)
- **List everything:** app servers, database (engine, version, size, daily change rate), batch jobs, reports and file shares.
- **Map every integration.** This is usually where the risk is:
  - EHR/ADT feeds and HL7 SIU scheduling messages
  - Interface engine (Mirth, Rhapsody, Cloverleaf)
  - Patient portal, SMS/email reminders, billing and registration
  - Call center telephony, kiosks, and any hardcoded IPs or hostnames
- **Find peak load times**, such as Monday 7–10 a.m. and open-scheduling days. Also find the lowest-traffic window.
- **Set targets:** how much data you can afford to lose at cutover (should be zero), how fast you must recover, and uptime commitments.
- **If it's a vendor product** (Epic Cadence, Oracle Health, Cerner, etc.), confirm early that the vendor supports your cloud and approach. This can change the whole plan.

## Phase 1: Compliance and Governance
- **Sign a Business Associate Agreement (BAA) with the cloud provider** (HIPAA, or your local equivalent). Use only services the provider lists as eligible for patient data.
- **Bring in early:** security/privacy officer, clinical operations leaders, IT change board, and the vendor.
- **Do a risk assessment** covering patient data flows, encryption at rest and in transit, key management, and audit logging.
- **Agree a change freeze** around cutover, and avoid go-live during flu season, holidays or EHR upgrades.

## Phase 2: Cloud Foundation
- **Private connectivity:** a dedicated link (Direct Connect, ExpressRoute or Interconnect) plus a backup VPN. Size it for CDC traffic plus live app traffic during the overlap period.
- **Identity:** single sign-on or AD integration so staff log in exactly as they do now.
- **Resilience:** run across multiple availability zones, with backups, monitoring, and central logging.
- **Infrastructure as code** (Terraform, Bicep, etc.) so environments can be rebuilt the same way every time.
- **Migration approach:** move the app as-is with a managed database. Don't redesign the app as part of this migration; that adds risk.

## Phase 3: Data Copying
1. **Initial full load** of the database to the cloud, using native backup/restore or DMS tools.
2. **Continuous change capture** to keep the cloud copy current (AWS DMS, Azure DMS, GoldenGate, Debezium or native replication).
3. **Check continuously:** row counts, checksums on key tables (appointments, slots, resources, patients), and replication lag.
4. **Prepare reverse replication** (cloud back to on-prem). This keeps on-prem current after cutover, so rolling back loses no data.

## Phase 4: Integrations
- **Send copies of HL7 traffic to the cloud environment in shadow mode.** The interface engine duplicates the messages and you compare the outputs without acting on them.
- **Make all integration endpoints switchable** by DNS or configuration change. Lower DNS TTLs to about 60 seconds at least 48 hours before cutover.
- **Watch for duplicates.** Reminders and outbound messages must not fire from both environments.

## Phase 5: Testing
- **Functional and regression testing**, plus clinical user acceptance testing with real schedulers, front desk staff and the call center.
- **Load testing at 1.5–2× your Monday-morning peak**, measuring response times from clinics over the new network path.
- **Failure testing:** lose an availability zone, lose the connectivity link, and confirm replication recovers.
- **Security:** penetration test and vulnerability scans.
- **Full rehearsal:** at least two complete practice cutovers and rollbacks in a staging copy, each timed.

## Phase 6: Cutover

| Time | Step |
|---|---|
| T-7 days | Go/no-go #1: replication lag consistently under 5 seconds, checks clean, testing signed off |
| T-48 hrs | DNS TTLs lowered; staff notified; downtime procedures (printed schedules, read-only reports) staged as a backup |
| T-0 (quiet window) | Put the app in read-only mode for bookings, or drain booking traffic |
| T+1–3 min | Confirm replication lag is zero; run final checksums |
| T+3–5 min | Point the app, load balancer and interface engine to the cloud; start reverse replication |
| T+5–10 min | Resume bookings; smoke-test booking, rescheduling, cancellation and HL7 output |
| T+1–4 hrs | Watch closely; go/no-go #2 to confirm the switch |

- **Optional extra caution:** move read-only traffic (reporting, portal viewing) to the cloud first, a week or two early.
- **Phasing by clinic or site** only works if no providers, rooms or other resources are shared across the split. Otherwise you risk double-booking.

## Phase 7: Rollback
- **Set rollback triggers in advance:** error rate or response-time thresholds, failed HL7 messages, or any data integrity problem.
- **Rolling back means pointing everything to on-prem again.** Because of reverse replication, no bookings are lost.
- **Time-box the rollback decision**, for example within 72 hours. After that, problems are fixed forward in the cloud.

## Phase 8: Stabilization and Shutdown
- **Support period of 2–4 weeks:** extra staff on hand and daily check-ins with clinic managers.
- **Keep on-prem running in the background for 30–90 days**, then archive it according to your records-retention rules.
- **Shut down the on-prem servers**, then update DR plans, runbooks and the BAA/vendor documents.

---

## Main Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Double-booking during the overlap | One database accepts bookings at a time; freeze bookings at the switch |
| A hidden integration breaks | Thorough discovery plus shadow-mode HL7 traffic |
| Slower response from clinics | Load-test over the real network path; dedicated link |
| Duplicate reminders sent | Disable outbound jobs in the inactive environment |
| Compliance gap | BAA, eligible services only, sign-off from the privacy officer |

**Typical timeline:** 4–6 months for a mid-size hospital, mostly spent on discovery, integrations and testing.
