# BUILD PLAN: Multi-tenant SaaS billing system

## 1. Classification and constraints
- **What:** A billing platform for an existing multi-tenant SaaS product. It covers the plan catalog, subscriptions, usage metering, rating, a double-entry ledger, invoicing, tax, payment collection, dunning, entitlements, and ERP export. It bills the product's tenants (B2B).
- **Type:** Software. The billing system is new, but it goes into an existing product (tenants, identity, cloud, and some current way of invoicing all already exist, ASSUMED). Not a small build: it touches money, outside customers, and personal data.
- **Dominant constraint:** Correctness, then compliance (PCI DSS, tax and invoicing law).
- **Worst failure:** Wrong charges at scale with money already moved before anyone notices: double charges, or one tenant's usage billed to another. V2, V7 and V8 guard against this.

**Budgets**

**Missing inputs.** The plan proceeds on these assumptions; each one changes the plan if false:
1. **Pricing model.** Assumed hybrid: seats plus metered usage. If it is pure seat-based subscriptions, V1 likely flips to "buy" and Phases 2–3 shrink to integration work. *This one changes the phase structure.*
2. **What "multi-tenant" means.** Assumed: we bill our own tenants. If instead we offer billing-as-a-service (tenants bill *their* customers), there is a second tenancy level, the PSP becomes a connected-accounts model, and merchant onboarding/KYC adds a phase before Phase 5.
3. **Legacy billing and volumes.** Assumed: legacy invoicing exists. If it doesn't, the shadow run in Phase 4 compares against Finance's hand-computed expected invoices, and Phase 7 is dropped.
4. **Business footprint.** Currencies, tax jurisdictions, selling legal entities, SOX applicability, and the ERP system (Finance/Legal).
5. **Peak usage event rate and tenant count** (Product Eng).

## 3. Tradeoff gates (resolved up front)

| Decision | Reversibility | Default (chosen now) | Assumption | Validated by | Flip condition |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Build vs buy | R3: once invoices and subscriptions live at a vendor, data gravity sets in | Buy the PSP (Stripe) and tax engine (Stripe Tax or Avalara). Build metering, rating, ledger, and invoicing | The hybrid pricing model fits bought engines poorly, and vendor fees over 3 years exceed build cost | V1 | V1 shows a bought engine (Stripe Billing, Orb, Metronome) expresses every plan with acceptable TCO. Then buy, and build only metering, entitlements, and integration |
| Tenancy and isolation | R3: one leak is permanent harm, and re-siloing means migrating all data | Shared Postgres. Every row and key carries `tenant_id`, with Postgres row-level security (RLS) enforced for the app role. Idempotency keys are scoped per tenant | No contract requires dedicated storage or data residency | V2 | V2 fails, or a contract requires a dedicated DB or residency. Then move to cells (a silo per tier or region) |
| Data model and identifiers | R3: issued invoices and ledger entries cannot be rewritten | Append-only double-entry ledger. Money as integer minor units plus ISO currency. Prices are immutable and versioned. Corrections are credit notes, never edits. IDs are ULIDs | Aggregated postings fit within a single Postgres primary's headroom | V3 | Posting load approaches primary limits during V3. Then shard the ledger by tenant |
| Invoice numbering | R3: once a number is issued it is permanent; EU VAT requires sequential numbering (Directive 2006/112/EC Art. 226) | Gapless sequence per legal entity, allocated at finalization inside the ledger transaction (not at draft) | One sequence per selling entity is legally enough | Cited basis (REQUIREMENT) + Tax adviser review in V6 | A jurisdiction requires a different series. Then add per-series sequences |
| Consistency vs availability | R3: money paths are customer-visible | Ledger, invoices, and payments are strongly consistent (single primary, serializable posting). Ingest favors availability: durable ack, at-least-once delivery, dedupe on the event key. Entitlements are eventually consistent from cache | Usage can lag minutes; money cannot diverge | V3, V4 | V4 shows dedupe can't hold exactness at peak. Then dedupe at ingest with a keyed store |
| Data-privacy boundary | R3: PCI scope and personal data | No card number (PAN) ever touches our systems; PSP-hosted fields only, so scope is SAQ A. Billing-contact PII sits in its own table with field-level KMS encryption and is never logged. Usage events carry no end-user PII | Only card and PSP-native payment methods are needed | V5 | A need arises to store PAN or run our own bank rails. Then re-scope PCI before building it |
| PSP and token custody | R3: stored payment methods live at the PSP | Stripe, behind our own payment-provider adapter (the seam keeps code rework to R2; it doesn't make tokens portable) | The PSP offers PCI-compliant card-data export (confirm in the Phase 0 contract) | Cited basis: contract clause | Export not contractually available. Then decide multi-PSP before go-live |
| Monolith vs services | R2: splitting modules later costs weeks | A modular monolith (billing core) plus a separate ingest service (it scales and fails differently) plus async workers | One team | Phase 1 module-boundary lint: no cross-module table access | More than 2 teams, or ingest scaling dominates core deploys |
| Sync vs async | R2 | Ingest goes through a durable log (managed Kafka). Every external side effect (PSP, email, ERP) goes through a transactional outbox. PSP webhooks are the source of truth for payment outcome | Outbox lag is acceptable | V4, V8 | Outbox lag breaks the invoice-run budget |
| Usage store | R2: re-aggregation migration | Raw events immutable in object storage (Parquet). Aggregates in partitioned Postgres | Peak rate is in the low thousands of events/s | V4 | V4 fails at peak ×3. Then move aggregates to ClickHouse |
| Migration and system of record | R3: real money and customer-visible | Legacy stays the system of record. Sync is one-way, legacy → new (import). Shadow billing runs for 2 cycles, then cutover by tenant cohort. After cutover, legacy keeps shadow-computing for those cohorts as a fallback. Decommission is the point of no return | The legacy data can be read and imported | V7, V9 | V7 cannot reach zero unexplained discrepancies. Then fix or re-scope before any cohort |

**R1 defaults:** language and framework → the team's existing stack; feature flags → the existing flag service; invoice PDF rendering → server-side HTML-to-PDF; email delivery → the existing transactional email provider; entitlement cache on a billing outage → fail open with last-known state for tenants in good standing.

**Inherited constraints** (not changed by this plan): the product's tenant identity and `tenant_id` format (changing it would mean a product-wide migration); product SSO; the existing cloud (ASSUMED AWS) and CI/CD.

**N/A:** prompt+RAG vs fine-tune, hosted vs self-hosted model: no AI component.

## 4. Walking skeleton (Phase 0)
- **The request:** The product backend emits one real usage event (`api_calls: 1`) for the company's own internal dogfood tenant. Then `GET /v1/tenants/{id}/invoices/upcoming` returns a draft invoice line priced at a stub flat unit price.
- **Tiers it crosses:** product backend → ingest service (workload-identity auth) → Kafka → aggregator worker → Postgres (usage aggregate, stub ledger posting) → rating stub → draft invoice → billing API → internal admin page. The PSP tier runs in **Stripe test mode only**: it creates the customer and a $0 dry-run invoice object. No money can move.
- **Deploy, logs, monitoring, rollback:** Terraform for infra. CI builds immutable images and runs forward-only migrations. One OpenTelemetry trace spans every tier, with structured logs that are PII-redacted. There is an alert on ingest error rate and consumer lag. One deliberate rollback is rehearsed through the pipeline.
- **Who can reach it:** Internal only. The ingest allow-list accepts the dogfood tenant, a feature flag gates the product emitter, and the admin page sits behind staff SSO.
- **Parallel Phase 0 tasks:**
  - Inventory everything that depends on legacy billing (each ASSUMED until confirmed).
  - Baseline legacy invoice volumes, run duration, and billing-ticket rate.
  - Collect missing inputs 1–5.
  - Start the organizational items: PSP production/KYB, tax vendor and DPA, a Finance owner for V6, a QSA or security reviewer for V5, legacy data access approval.
  - Run V1.

Exit check: **V0**.

## 5. Phases

- **Phase 1: Tenancy, ledger, and contracts** (the widest rework surface)
  - Unlocks: specification of the metering and invoicing domain.
  - Depends on: V0; V1 (if V1 flips to buy, this phase shrinks to tenancy plus vendor integration and the plan is re-cut).
  - Tasks:
    1. Tenant model, RLS, and tenant-scoped keys; run V2.
    2. Ledger schema, posting API with idempotency keys, and invariants; run V3.
    3. Versioned catalog and price schema.
    4. Usage event contract v1 (schema registry; this is the contract product teams emit against).
    5. Public billing API contract, OpenAPI v1.
    6. Transactional outbox.
    7. Postgres multi-AZ, PITR, and a restore drill that proves RPO 0 and RTO 1 h.
  - Rollback: expand/contract migrations; there is no real data yet, so schema reset is still allowed until Phase 4 imports.
  - Exit check: V2 and V3 pass; the restore drill meets RPO/RTO; the module-boundary lint is green.

- **Phase 2: Usage metering**
  - Unlocks: trustworthy usage totals for rating.
  - Depends on: Phase 1, V2, the peak-rate input.
  - Tasks:
    1. Producer SDK with local buffering and event keys.
    2. Ingest validation and per-tenant rate limits.
    3. Dedupe and late-arrival window (design parameter: 72 h).
    4. Aggregation, plus a raw-event archive with replay.
    5. Dead-letter queue (DLQ) and a reconciliation of producer counts against ingested counts.
    6. Run V4.
  - Rollback: the product emitter flag goes off; raw events can be replayed into a fresh aggregate.
  - Exit check: V4 passes; aggregates re-derived from the raw archive match exactly.

- **Phase 3: Subscriptions, rating, invoicing** (drafts only; nothing sent externally)
  - Unlocks: complete invoices that can be compared against legacy.
  - Depends on: Phase 2, V3, V4; the jurisdiction list for retention and tax.
  - Tasks:
    1. Subscription lifecycle: create, upgrade, downgrade, cancel, proration.
    2. Rating engine as a pure function of (price version, usage aggregate, subscription state).
    3. Tax engine in sandbox.
    4. Finalization with gapless numbering and credit notes.
    5. Checkpointed, resumable invoice run.
    6. Golden corpus in CI; run V6.
  - Rollback: drafts are disposable; re-rating is deterministic.
  - Exit check: V6 passes; the invoice run meets 6 h at 3× the tenant count recorded in the Phase 0 baseline.

- **Phase 4: Legacy import and shadow billing**
  - Unlocks: evidence that the new invoices are right.
  - Depends on: Phase 3, V6, legacy data access approval.
  - Tasks:
    1. Idempotent one-way import (legacy → new) of tenants, subscriptions, and prices, run nightly.
    2. Shadow invoices for **all** tenants, never sent (the email sender has an allow-list).
    3. Daily diff report classifying each discrepancy (new-system bug / legacy bug acknowledged by Finance / rule difference).
    4. Shadow entitlement checks compared against the product's current plan checks.
    5. Run V7 over 2 cycles.
  - Rollback: delete the imported data and re-import; legacy is untouched and remains the system of record.
  - Exit check: V7 passes.

- **Phase 5: Payments, collections, reconciliation**
  - Build overlaps the Phase 4 run. Nothing is exposed until Phase 6.
  - Unlocks: the ability to collect money safely.
  - Depends on: Phase 3, the PSP production account, the PSP adapter contract.
  - Tasks:
    1. PSP adapter.
    2. Charge executor with idempotency key = invoice ID + attempt.
    3. Webhook ingestion with signature verification.
    4. Refunds and credit-note-to-refund flow.
    5. Dunning schedule.
    6. Anomaly circuit breaker: halt a run if its total deviates more than 20% from the previous cycle (ASSUMPTION; Finance to tune).
    7. Daily PSP reconciliation.
    8. ERP journal export.
    9. PSP-hosted payment-method capture.
    10. Run V5 and V8.
  - Rollback: the charge-executor flag goes off; PSP stays in test mode.
  - Exit check: V5 and V8 pass; a test-mode ERP export reconciles to the ledger.

- **Phase 6: Cohort cutover** (exposure smallest first)
  - Unlocks: the new system becomes the system of record per cohort.
  - Depends on: V2, V5, V6, V7, V8; tax registrations; billing on-call staffed.
  - Waves: internal tenant (live charge on a company card) → 5 friendly tenants → 10% → 50% → 100%. Each wave lasts one full cycle.
  - Gate to widen, per wave: 0 unexplained discrepancies against legacy shadow; 100% PSP reconciliation; billing-ticket rate ≤ the Phase 0 BASELINE.
  - Within waves: product entitlements switch to the new system for each cohort; the tenant portal (invoice download, PSP-hosted payment update, plan view) opens to cut-over cohorts only; pen test before the 10% wave.
  - Rollback per cohort: flip the system-of-record flag back to legacy, which is still shadow-computing; correct any wrong charge by credit note and refund.
  - Exit check: 100% of tenants cut over for one clean cycle; SLO dashboards meet the §1 targets.

- **Phase 7: Legacy decommission** (point of no return)
  - Depends on: Phase 6 and **V9**.
  - Tasks:
    1. Repoint or retire every legacy dependent from the Phase 0 inventory.
    2. Export legacy data to an encrypted archive per retention rules; test a restore.
    3. Revoke legacy credentials.
    4. Shut down.
  - Rollback: none after shutdown. Before it, re-enable legacy for any cohort.
  - Exit check: V9 passes, and no access attempts against legacy for 30 days.

## 10. Deliberately deferred
- **Revenue recognition (ASC 606) schedules:** pulled forward if Finance needs them from billing rather than the ERP.
- **Second currency or selling entity:** the data model supports both from Phase 1; enabling either waits for the first sale in that currency or entity.
- **Cells, dedicated tenant storage, data residency:** pulled forward by a contract requirement, or by V2 failing.
- **Ledger sharding / ClickHouse:** pulled forward if V3 or V4 headroom runs out.
- **Sales-led deal structures (commits, prepaid credits, ramps) and CPQ/quoting:** pulled forward when sales signs the first such deal.
- **ACH/SEPA/wire payment methods:** pulled forward by tenant demand, after a PCI and scope review.
- **Cloud marketplace billing (AWS/GCP):** pulled forward by the first marketplace listing.
- **Tenant self-serve plan changes beyond basics, real-time spend alerts:** pulled forward after the 100% wave, when billing-ticket data shows demand.
- **MRR and churn analytics:** stays in BI/ERP until Finance needs it inside billing.
