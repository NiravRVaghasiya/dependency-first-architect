# Plan: Customer-support RAG chatbot over help-center docs

## 1. Classification and constraints
- **What:** A chat widget on the help center. It answers customer questions only from published help-center articles, cites its sources, and hands off to a human (as a ticket) when the docs don't cover the question.
- **Type:** An AI system plus ordinary software. It's a new system, but it sits on existing systems: the help-center CMS, the ticketing system and the website. This is not a small build, because outside users see it and they will type personal data into it.
- **Dominant constraint:** Correctness. Every answer must be backed by a current article.
- **Worst failure:** The bot states a policy (refunds, billing, cancellation, security steps) with confidence, but the policy is wrong, made up or out of date, and customers act on it. Courts have held a company to its chatbot's misstatement (Moffatt v. Air Canada, 2024 BCCRT 149), and once an answer is given it can't be taken back. V3 guards this, and V5 re-checks it on live traffic. The next-worst failure is customer personal data leaking through transcripts, traces or the model vendor (V4).

**Missing inputs.** The phase order rests on assumptions 1 and 2:
1. **Scope.** I assume the bot only answers questions and hands off, with no account lookups or actions (refunds, plan changes). *If that's wrong:* add sign-in, per-action human approval and tool-call containment as a gated phase before any exposure, and re-plan from Step 1. The worst failure then becomes an unauthorized action.
2. **What gets indexed.** I assume only published, public articles. *If internal-only or plan-specific articles are included:* access control at retrieval time becomes a security dependency before exposure, and becomes part of V2.
3. **Existing bot.** I assume none (for example, no decision-tree bot) is being replaced. *If one exists:* it becomes the fallback, and switching it off becomes a gated irreversible step.
4. Which help-center platform and ticketing system you use (their APIs and webhooks), and whether the vendor's own AI agent is an option (see build vs buy).
5. Regions served and languages. I assume English first. If you serve EU users, telling users they're talking to an AI is a REQUIREMENT under EU AI Act Art. 50(1); legal to confirm.
6. Human cost per ticket, current CSAT, and traffic volume.

**minor defaults:**
- One service with two processes (API and ingestion worker), not microservices.
- Answers stream synchronously; ingestion runs asynchronously.
- The CMS is the source of truth. The index catches up in the background, but unpublished articles are removed fast (webhook updates plus a nightly full reconcile).
- pgvector in managed Postgres, not a dedicated vector database.
- Embedding model and chunker are pinned per index version. Re-embedding is hours, assuming ≤ ~10k articles.
- Handoff creates a ticket in your existing system.
- Conversation memory lasts for one session only: stored server-side and expired after a set time.

**N/A:** Migration, cutover and system-of-record rows, because no existing bot is being replaced (see missing input 3).

## 4. First end-to-end slice (Phase 0)
- **The request:** A staff member asks "How do I reset my password?" in the widget. The answer streams back with a link to the real article it came from. "Talk to a human" creates a ticket in a staff-only test queue.
- **Tiers it crosses:**
  1. Widget on the live help center.
  2. Chat API.
  3. Retrieval from pgvector over real published articles (simple chunking).
  4. Hosted model call: system instructions are kept separate from user and retrieved text, which go in only as data, and the hard token and cost caps from §1 apply.
  5. Answer rendered as plain text plus citation links only.
  6. Ticketing API.
- **Deployed, logged, monitored and rolled back:** CI builds a pinned container. Terraform deploys it to a managed container platform behind a feature flag. OpenTelemetry traces each turn end to end, and each trace records the prompt version, model ID and index version. Dashboards and alerts cover token use, cost and latency. Rollback means turning the flag off and redeploying the previous image, and it is rehearsed once.
- **Who can reach it:** Staff only, on an allow-list behind company sign-in and the flag. No customer traffic.
- **Exit check (V0):**
  - A real question succeeds with one trace across all six tiers.
  - A deploy and a rollback both succeed through the pipeline.
  - An injected model-endpoint failure (bad API key) fires an alert, and the widget shows the fallback reply.
  - First values for latency, per-turn cost and tokens are recorded as baselines (a floor at near-zero load).

## 5. Phases

- **Phase 1: Defenses and budgets**
  - Unlocks: Safe work on capabilities; the defense half of the exposure gates.
  - Depends on: V0.
  - Tasks, in order:
    1. Trace and log store with redaction at write time, restricted access and a provisional 30-day retention (replaced when V4 sets the real period).
    2. Output renderer that allows only help-center links and no images or other URLs, so nothing can be leaked through a link.
    3. PII redaction before the model call.
    4. Input and output moderation, and an instruction to stay on topic.
    5. Rate limits per session and per IP.
    6. Hard caps enforced in code; a cut-off for runaway sessions; timeout leading to the fallback reply.
    7. High-risk topic detector that routes the question to a human.
    8. AI-disclosure banner.
    9. Injection test suite in CI: ≥ 150 cases, planted both in user input and in documents in a test index.
  - Rollback: Guardrail config is versioned; revert the config.
  - Exit check: V1 passes. A synthetic runaway session is cut off at the cap and emits the cost metric.

- **Phase 2: Retrieval** (may overlap Phase 1; nothing in it is exposed)
  - Unlocks: Specifying the answer prompt (V2).
  - Depends on: Phase 0; the labeled question set (§2).
  - Tasks, in order:
    1. Chunk schema with the full metadata listed in §2.
    2. Ingestion: CMS API → clean HTML → chunks split at headings. Only published, public articles; visibility is also filtered at query time.
    3. Embeddings plus keyword (BM25) hybrid search.
    4. Webhook updates, fast removal of unpublished articles, nightly reconcile against CMS article counts.
    5. Index versions you can switch between (old version stays available).
    6. Assemble and redact the question set.
    7. Run V2.
    8. Build-vs-buy check: score the vendor's native agent on the same set.
  - Rollback: Switch back to the previous index version.
  - Exit check: V2 passes, and the build-vs-buy result is recorded against §3.

- **Phase 3: Model access, session memory, answer pipeline**
  - Unlocks: Exposure gates V3 and V4; the load test.
  - Depends on: V1, V2.
  - Tasks, in order:
    1. Model interface with retries, backoff and a fallback model.
    2. Structured output: the answer, its citations, and whether it declined.
    3. Citation check: cited articles must be in the retrieved set, otherwise the bot declines.
    4. Answer-only-from-docs prompt in a versioned prompt registry.
    5. Session memory (last N turns, server-side, expiring) and rewriting follow-up questions so they stand alone.
    6. A single retrieve-then-generate chain, nothing more.
    7. Handoff ticket with the redacted transcript and its citations.
    8. Eval harness in CI. An LLM judge is calibrated to expert labels for catching regressions, but V3 passes only on expert review.
    9. Run V3.
    10. Load test at the throughput target, once it is known (§1).
  - Rollback: Prompt and model versions live in config; revert them.
  - Exit check: V3 passes. The fallback model passes the eval at V3 thresholds through a config switch alone. The load test meets the latency budget. Operational complexity is still within §1.

- **Phase 4: Shadow mode, then canary** (exposure starts small)
  - Unlocks: Real-traffic evidence for V5.
  - Depends on: V1, V3, V4; staffing and legal items (§2).
  - Tasks, in order:
    1. Shadow mode: the bot drafts answers to real incoming chats or tickets, and agents see the drafts. Customers see nothing.
    2. Canary of 1% of help-center visitors, then 5%, in one locale. Visitors are assigned to the canary by a fixed rule and the assignment is logged.
    3. Daily expert review of sampled transcripts.
    4. Kill switch.
    5. Thumbs up/down and "this was wrong" capture (stored only, not yet used).
    6. Run V5 for 2 weeks at 5%.
  - Rollback: Set the flag to 0%, and visitors get the existing help center. There is no point of no return, but disclosure can't be undone, which is why V3 and V4 must pass first.
  - Exit check: V5 passes, and SLIs are recorded: success rate, first-token time, decline rate, handoff rate.

- **Phase 5: Ramp and feedback**
  - Unlocks: General availability; more locales.
  - Depends on: V5; the availability target (§1).
  - Tasks, in order:
    1. Ramp 5% → 25% → 50% → 100%. Each step holds for ≥ 3 days and stops automatically on burn-rate or quality regressions.
    2. Feedback loop: thumbs-down answers and handoffs go to support review. Reviewed cases join the question set (versioned), and doc gaps go to the content team.
    3. Red-team re-runs on a schedule.
    4. Verify the retention deletion job.
  - Rollback: Step back to the previous ramp percentage.
  - Exit check: At 100% over 30 days, the SLO is met. The next eval run after feedback improves the target metric with no regressions.

## 10. Deferred
- **Account lookups and actions:** pulled forward when the business asks for them. That means re-planning from Step 1, with sign-in, per-action approval enforced by the tool executor, and tool-call containment gated before exposure.
- **Internal articles, or an agent-assist copilot over them:** pulled forward when access control at retrieval time is built and added to V2.
- **Memory across sessions:** pulled forward on a product need. It requires a new privacy decision (R3) first.
- **Routing to a cheaper model:** pulled forward when V5 shows cost over budget while quality has room to spare on the eval set.
- **Fine-tuning:** pulled forward when V3 still fails on tone or format after prompt work, and only on non-personal data.
- **More locales:** pulled forward when locale 1 passes V5 and an expert-labeled question set exists for the next locale.
- **Self-hosted model:** pulled forward when V4 fails for every vendor, or when cost at scale breaks the V5 budget.
- **Other channels (in-app, email, voice):** pulled forward after general availability on the web, each with its own canary.
