# Multi-Tenant SaaS Billing System: Architecture

"Multi-tenant billing" can mean two things:

- **A.** You run a multi-tenant SaaS product and need to bill your own customers (the tenants).
- **B.** You're building a billing platform, and your tenants use it to bill *their* customers.

This design covers **A** and notes where **B** adds work. The core design is the same for both. B adds a second layer of tenancy and makes the isolation requirements stricter.

---

## 1. Requirements

**Functional**
- A product catalog with plans, prices, add-ons and versioning
- Pricing models: flat, per-seat, tiered/volume, usage-based, hybrid, minimum commits, prepaid credits
- Subscription lifecycle: trial → active → past_due → canceled/paused, plus upgrades, downgrades and proration
- Usage metering at high volume
- Invoicing, tax, multiple currencies, credit notes, refunds
- Payment collection, retries and dunning
- Entitlements, i.e. what a tenant is allowed to use right now
- Self-serve portal, admin tools, and webhooks or events for downstream systems

**Non-functional**
- **Correctness above everything else.** Money must never be created or lost, and every number must be auditable.
- Idempotency everywhere, since retries are normal
- Tenant isolation, both for data and for noisy-neighbor performance
- PCI DSS scope kept as small as possible
- Usage ingestion of more than 10k events/sec, with tolerance for late or duplicate events
- Fast entitlement checks (p99 < 10 ms), because they sit on your product's hot path

## 3. Core Domain Model

```
Tenant (account)
 ├── BillingProfile (currency, tax IDs, address, timezone, invoice settings)
 ├── PaymentMethod (PSP token only, never raw PAN)
 ├── Subscription
 │    ├── status, billing_anchor, current_period_start/end, cancel_at
 │    └── SubscriptionItem → PriceVersion (quantity for seats)
 ├── UsageEvent (raw) → UsageAggregate (per meter, per period)
 ├── Invoice (draft | open | paid | void | uncollectible)
 │    └── InvoiceLine (price_version_id, qty, unit_amount, period, tax lines)
 ├── CreditNote, CreditGrant (prepaid/promotional credits)
 └── LedgerAccount (AR, cash, credits, deferred revenue …)

Product → Price → PriceVersion (immutable once used)
Meter (event_name, aggregation: sum|count|max|unique_count|last)
```

### Invariants

1. **Money is stored as integer minor units plus an ISO currency code.** Never use floats. Use decimals with explicit rounding rules for sub-cent unit prices such as $0.0004/API call, and round once at the invoice line.
2. **Price versions are immutable.** Changing a price creates a new version. Existing subscriptions keep their version until they are migrated on purpose. Grandfathering then works by default.
3. **Finalized invoices are immutable.** Corrections are made with credit notes or a new invoice, never by editing.
4. **The ledger is the source of truth for balances.** It is double-entry and append-only, and every entry has a `source_type/source_id` (invoice, payment, refund, credit note). Balances are derived from it and can be cached, but they are always rebuildable.
5. **Every write API accepts an `Idempotency-Key`.** Store `(tenant_id, key) → response` for at least 24 hours.

## 4. Multi-Tenancy Strategy

| Concern | Approach |
|---|---|
| Data isolation | `tenant_id` on every row, enforced with **Postgres Row-Level Security** plus a tenant-scoped DB session (`SET app.tenant_id`). Defense in depth: also enforce it in the repository layer. |
| Deployment model | **Pooled** by default (shared schema). **Silo** option (dedicated DB/cell) for enterprise or regulated tenants. Abstract this behind a tenant→cell routing table from day one. |
| Noisy neighbors | Per-tenant rate limits at the gateway. Usage Kafka topics partitioned by `tenant_id`, with hot tenants moved to dedicated partitions. Per-tenant concurrency caps in the billing scheduler. |
| Configuration | Per-tenant settings: billing timezone, invoice numbering sequence, dunning policy, tax behavior, branding. |
| Encryption | Encrypt PII at rest. For Model B, use per-tenant data keys (KMS envelope encryption) so a tenant can be crypto-shredded. |
| Model B extras | Two-level tenancy (`platform_tenant_id`, `customer_id`), per-tenant PSP credentials (e.g. Stripe Connect accounts), per-tenant webhooks and API keys, and strict per-tenant export/audit. |

**Cell architecture at scale:** group tenants into cells. Each cell has its own DB, workers and Kafka partitions, with a global control plane for routing and the catalog. This limits the blast radius of an outage and lets you scale horizontally.

## 5. Key Flows

### 5.1 Usage Metering Pipeline
```
Product → POST /usage {tenant, meter, value, timestamp, idempotency_key}
       → validate + enqueue to Kafka (ack fast, durable)
       → Dedup worker (key: tenant+idempotency_key, Redis/RocksDB w/ TTL ≥ late window)
       → Aggregation store (ClickHouse): aggregates by tenant/meter/hour
       → At period close: Rating Engine queries aggregates for [period_start, period_end)
```
- **Event time vs. ingest time:** bill on event time. Allow a configurable **late-arrival window** (e.g. 24–72 hours) before the period is finalized. Events that arrive later go into the next invoice as adjustments rather than mutating a closed one.
- Keep raw events in S3/Parquet so you can **re-rate** after pricing bugs and handle customer disputes ("show me my calls").
- Show customers near-real-time usage from the aggregates, labeled as an estimate.

### 5.2 Billing Clock (Scheduler)
- Each subscription has `next_action_at`. Sharded workers poll with `SELECT … FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED WHERE next_action_at <= now()`, or use a durable workflow engine such as **Temporal**. Temporal is a strong fit for long-running lifecycle logic like trials, dunning and scheduled changes.
- Actions: renew, end trial, apply a scheduled plan change, close a usage period, finalize a draft invoice, run a dunning step.
- **Anchor dates:** store them in UTC and compute periods in the billing profile's timezone. Clamp month-end dates (a Jan 31 anchor becomes Feb 28/29, then returns to Mar 31).
- **Test clocks:** let a subscription run against simulated time. This is essential for testing a year of lifecycle behavior in seconds.

### 5.3 Invoice Generation
1. Create a **draft** invoice with recurring lines (in advance), usage lines (in arrears), and proration lines from mid-cycle changes.
2. Apply discounts, then credits (credit grants draw down in a defined priority order and by expiration).
3. Call the tax service and **snapshot the tax calculation** on the invoice, so a later recalculation never changes history.
4. Keep the draft open for a review window (e.g. 1 hour, or longer for enterprise/manual review).
5. **Finalize:** assign a sequential invoice number (gapless per tenant/legal entity where law requires it), render the PDF, post ledger entries (Dr AR / Cr Revenue or Deferred Revenue / Cr Tax Payable), and emit `invoice.finalized`.
6. Trigger payment if the subscription auto-charges. Otherwise send the invoice with net terms.

### 5.4 Proration
- Pick **one** policy per tenant and document it: `create_prorations` (credit unused time, charge new time), `none`, or `always_invoice` (bill immediately).
- Compute proration by seconds, or by days in the billing timezone, against the **old and new price versions**.
- Downgrades usually take effect at period end. Upgrades take effect immediately. Make this a config setting, not code.

### 5.5 Payments and Dunning
```
Invoice open → PaymentIntent (idempotency key = invoice_id + attempt_no)
  → PSP → sync result OR async webhook
Webhook → Inbox table (unique on psp_event_id) → processor → ledger + invoice status
```
- **Inbox pattern** for PSP webhooks: dedupe on event ID and process in order per object, so out-of-order delivery is handled.
- **Outbox pattern** for publishing your own events: write them in the same DB transaction as the state change, then relay them.
- **Dunning:** configurable retry schedule (e.g. days 1, 3, 5, 7). Retry smartly by decline code: never retry hard declines, and use the PSP's smart retry and card account updater where available. Send emails at each step and set `past_due` → grace period → restricted entitlements → `canceled`/`unpaid`.
- **Payment method abstraction:** cards, ACH/SEPA debit (asynchronous, with settlement delays and returns), wire/bank transfer with reconciliation against virtual account numbers.

### 5.6 Entitlements
- The product **never calls billing synchronously** to ask "can tenant X use feature Y?" It reads from the Entitlements service, which is fed by subscription events and cached in Redis or in-process with a short TTL.
- Fail-open vs. fail-closed is decided per feature. Usually fail-open for core access and fail-closed for expensive overages.
- Usage limits such as hard caps on prepaid credits use a fast counter (Redis) that is reconciled against the authoritative aggregates.

## 6. Data Storage

| Data | Store | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Catalog, subscriptions, invoices, ledger | **PostgreSQL** (RLS, serializable txns where needed) | Strong consistency and relational integrity |
| Raw usage events | **Kafka** → **S3/Parquet** | Durable, cheap, replayable |
| Usage aggregates | **ClickHouse** (or Druid/Pinot) | Fast time-window aggregation at volume |
| Idempotency/dedup keys, entitlement cache | **Redis** | Low latency, TTL |
| Invoice PDFs | Object storage (S3), immutable | Legal retention |
| Analytics / RevRec | Warehouse (Snowflake/BigQuery) via CDC | Kept off the transactional path |

Ledger schema sketch:
```sql
ledger_entries(
  id, tenant_id, txn_id, account_id, direction CHECK (direction IN ('debit','credit')),
  amount_minor BIGINT CHECK (amount_minor > 0), currency CHAR(3),
  source_type, source_id, effective_at, created_at
)
-- Invariant enforced per txn_id: SUM(debits) = SUM(credits) per currency
```

## 7. Reliability and Correctness Practices

- **Reconciliation jobs (daily):** ledger vs. PSP balance transactions and payouts; invoices vs. payments; usage raw counts vs. aggregates. Alert on any non-zero difference.
- **Exactly-once effects** come from idempotency keys plus unique constraints plus the inbox/outbox pattern. Don't rely on exactly-once delivery.
- **Rehearsal:** before a pricing migration, re-rate the last period in shadow mode and diff the invoices.
- **Audit log:** every admin action (manual credit, invoice void, plan override) records who did it and why, and the log is immutable.
- **Kill switches:** pause auto-charging globally or per tenant, and pause invoice finalization during an incident.
- **SLOs:** ingestion durability (no lost events), invoice finalization within N minutes of period end, and entitlement p99 latency.

## 8. Security and Compliance

- **PCI:** collect cards with the PSP's hosted fields or elements and store only tokens. This keeps you at SAQ A rather than full PCI scope.
- **SOC 2 / GDPR:** tenant data export and deletion, while keeping financial records for their legal retention period (pseudonymize instead of deleting invoices).
- **Tax:** use a dedicated provider. Keep the tax ID, place of supply, and exemption certificates per customer. E-invoicing (EU, India, LATAM) is a per-region adapter.
- **RBAC:** separate permissions for viewing billing, managing payment methods, and issuing credits or refunds. Refunds above a threshold require two-person approval.

---

## 9. Downstream Integrations

- **Revenue recognition (ASC 606/IFRS 15):** emit performance-obligation data (service period per line) so revenue is recognized over time. This is often handled by a separate RevRec tool fed from the ledger.
- **Accounting:** sync journal summaries to NetSuite or QuickBooks, not individual events.
- **CRM:** sync MRR/ARR, plan and status to Salesforce/HubSpot.
- **Tenant-facing webhooks** (essential for Model B): signed (HMAC), retried with backoff, replayable from a dashboard.

## 10. Build vs. Buy

Billing looks simple and isn't. Proration, tax, dunning, revenue recognition and reconciliation each take serious effort.

| Situation | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Standard seat/flat plans | **Buy:** Stripe Billing, Chargebee, Recurly |
| Heavy usage-based pricing | **Buy a usage-native tool:** Orb, Metronome, Lago (open source) |
| Billing *is* your product (Model B), or extreme scale or unusual pricing | **Build** the core (catalog, subscriptions, rating, ledger) and **buy** the edges (PSP, tax, RevRec) |

A common hybrid is to build your own **metering and entitlements** layer, since it's tightly coupled to your product, and push rated usage into a bought billing engine.

## 11. Phased Rollout

1. **MVP:** catalog with versioning, flat/seat subscriptions, PSP integration with webhook inbox, invoices, basic dunning, ledger, entitlements.
2. **Usage:** ingestion pipeline, meters, rating, late-arrival handling, usage dashboards.
3. **Enterprise:** custom contracts, commits and minimums, net terms and invoicing, multi-currency, tax across regions, silo tenancy.
4. **Scale and finance:** cells, reconciliation automation, RevRec feed, e-invoicing, re-rating tooling.
