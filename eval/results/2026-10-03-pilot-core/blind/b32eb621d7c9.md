# BUILD PLAN: Multi-tenant SaaS billing system

## 1. Classification and constraints
- **What:** The billing subsystem of a multi-tenant B2B SaaS product. It covers the plan catalog, subscriptions, metered usage, rating, invoicing, payments, tax, dunning, reconciliation, and a tenant-facing billing view. It bills **our tenants** (customer organizations) for subscriptions plus usage.
- **Type:** Software, not AI. It is a new service plugged into an existing product (identity, tenant directory, usage sources). It replaces an assumed manual invoicing process, so the brownfield rules apply to the cutover. This is not a small build: it moves money and holds tenant billing data.
- **Dominant constraint:** Correctness (money), then compliance.
- **Worst failure:** Money moves wrongly and we can't undo it: a tenant is double-charged, charged the wrong amount, or charged for another tenant's usage, and the ledger, the invoices and the payment provider stop agreeing. V4 guards this, backed by V3 and V8.

**Budgets**

**Missing inputs** (most plan-changing first):
1. **Who pays whom.** I assumed we bill our own tenants. If instead tenants use this system to bill *their* customers (billing as a service), several things change:
   - We need a merchant onboarding phase (Stripe Connect, KYC) before Phase 3.
   - Each tenant needs its own tax registrations.
   - The public API and webhooks become an R3 contract.
   - The catalog becomes tenant-configurable.
2. **The price book**: seats, usage, prepaid credits, negotiated contracts. This decides V1. If prepaid credits must be drawn down in real time, the consistency row flips.
3. **The current billing setup and tenant count.** I assumed manual invoicing through an accounting tool. If there is no current billing (a new product), V8 becomes a synthetic suite of known-correct invoices and Phase 6 is moot. If a homegrown or vendor engine is live, Phase 4 also has to migrate active subscriptions.
4. Jurisdictions, currencies and issuing legal entities. These drive tax (V7), invoice numbering, retention and residency (V2).
5. Audit scope (SOC 2, or SOX if a public company). Under SOX, change-approval controls block Phase 0.

## 3. Tradeoff gates (resolved up front)

| Decision | Reversibility | Default (chosen now) | Assumption | Validated by | Flip condition |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Build vs buy | R3: rework takes months, and the data stays with the vendor | **Buy** payments (Stripe, hosted card fields), tax (Stripe Tax or Avalara) and email. **Build** catalog, subscriptions, rating, invoicing and an append-only ledger as our own system of record | The price book mixes usage and negotiated contracts that hosted engines handle only with manual workarounds | V1 | V1 shows the full price book bills correctly in a hosted engine (Stripe Billing, Orb) at acceptable fees. Then buy the engine, keep our own metering, and demote our ledger to a reconciliation mirror |
| Tenancy and isolation | R3: a leak of billing data can't be undone, and re-keying later means months of work | Shared Postgres. `tenant_id` on every row and in every composite key and foreign key. Row-level security (RLS) with the tenant set per transaction from the verified token, failing closed. Bill-run workers open tenant-scoped sessions | RLS covers the API, jobs and exports, and its overhead is acceptable | V3 | V3 fails, or a contract requires dedicated storage. Then that tenant gets its own silo (schema or database per tenant) |
| Consistency vs availability | R3: what a balance or payment state means is customer-visible | Ledger favors consistency: a single primary, serializable posting, append-only, corrections by credit note only. Usage ingestion favors availability: at-least-once delivery with dedupe by `event_id`. Entitlements are served from a last-known cache | Ledger writes are low-volume | Basis: even 100k tenants × 20 entries over a 4 h run is about 140 writes/s, more than 10× under one Postgres primary. V4 covers correctness | A real-time balance requirement (prepaid credits drawn per request), or bill-run writes above 1,000/s. Then a balance service with reservations, or partitioning |
| Data model and identifiers | R3: invoices are legal documents once issued | Money as integer minor units plus ISO 4217 currency. Rated usage held as `numeric`, rounded once per line (rule set by finance). Prices immutable and versioned, with subscriptions pinned to a price version. ULIDs internally. Gapless sequential invoice numbers per issuing entity, assigned at finalization. `home_region` on the tenant from day one | Finance accepts the rounding rule | V4 (invariants), V8 (parity proves the rounding) | V8 finds systematic rounding differences. Change the rule before the first real invoice; until then it is cheap |
| Data-privacy boundary | R3: an exposure can't be undone | No card numbers ever reach us: hosted fields or Checkout keep us at PCI SAQ A (reduced scope, not zero). Billing contact details, addresses and VAT IDs live only in the billing database, encrypted and tenant-scoped. Usage events carry only tenant, meter, quantity, timestamp and event ID; the schema rejects anything else. Logs are redacted | No flow needs card numbers on our side | V6, V2 | Any flow needs card numbers (phone orders, for example). Re-scope PCI before building it |
| Data residency | R3: data at rest in the wrong place breaches the obligation | One region, co-located with the product | Billing data follows product data, and no tenant contract requires EU residency | V2 (DPO sign-off) | A legal or contractual residency obligation. Then regional cells, routed by the `home_region` seam |
| Monolith vs services | R2: coordinated change across modules | A modular monolith billing service, plus a separate usage-ingestion pipeline (very different scale profile) | One team owns it | Import-lint rules enforce module boundaries in CI from Phase 1 | A second team owns a module, or a module needs to scale on its own |
| Sync vs async | R2: retry semantics are spread through all code | Money movement is async: transactional outbox, then payment-provider calls with idempotency keys. Provider webhooks are the source of truth for payment status. The bill run is an idempotent batch per (tenant, period). Only previews and entitlement reads are sync | Nothing needs instant payment confirmation | V4 | Self-serve signup needs instant confirmation. Then confirm the payment synchronously, while the webhook stays authoritative |
| Usage transport | R2: replaceable because the raw store can be replayed | Managed Kafka (MSK or Confluent) into immutable Parquet/Iceberg on S3 | Fits the peak rate | V5 | V5 fails at 2× peak, or operating cost exceeds the Phase 2 check |
| (Brownfield) System of record and cutover | R2 per tenant (one cycle to revert), but invoices already issued are permanent | Manual billing stays the system of record for each tenant until its wave goes live; after that, this service. Sync runs one way, billing to the general ledger (GL). Order: shadow run, then waves, then decommission | The manual process can keep running in parallel for 2+ cycles | V8, V9 | A plan type fails parity. That plan type stays manual |

**R1 defaults:** entitlements fail open with the last-known cache; PDF rendering library; email provider; admin UI framework; feature-flag tool (Unleash or LaunchDarkly); migration tool (Atlas or Flyway, expand/contract only).

**N/A:** prompt+RAG vs fine-tune, and hosted vs self-hosted model: no AI component. Migration strategy (rehost/replatform/refactor): we are replacing a manual process, not moving a system.

## 4. Walking skeleton (Phase 0)
- **Request:** An internal operator runs "bill tenant `internal-dogfood` for period P". The response is a finalized invoice of **$1.00**, paid with a **company-owned card**, a refund issued, and an invoice email sent to an internal mailbox. The tenant's real product also sends one usage event, which appears as a $0 line on that invoice.
- **Tiers crossed:**
  1. Admin CLI/UI, through the API gateway with IdP authentication, into the billing service.
  2. Postgres (tenant, subscription, ledger entry, invoice).
  3. The outbox worker calls Stripe in **live mode**. The card is entered on the provider's hosted page, so no card number touches us.
  4. The signed webhook returns to the receiver, which posts a ledger payment entry and marks the invoice paid.
  5. The email is sent.
  6. Usage path: product emitter, ingestion endpoint, Kafka, raw store on S3, aggregation, invoice line.
- **Deploy, log, monitor, roll back:**
  - Deploy: Terraform provisions the infrastructure. GitHub Actions builds a pinned image, runs migrations (expand-only) and deploys to ECS or Kubernetes.
  - Log and trace: OpenTelemetry traces cross HTTP, the outbox and the webhook; the invoice ID travels through as payment-provider metadata to tie the async legs together. JSON logs carry `tenant_id`, a correlation ID and the data classification, and pass through a PII redaction filter.
  - Alerts: error rate, outbox lag, webhook signature failures, consumer lag.
  - Rollback: redeploy the previous image. This is safe because migrations only add.
- **Who can reach it:** Only the internal tenant, on an allow-list behind a flag. The only money moved is ours, a nominal amount, and it is refunded.
- **Also started in Phase 0 (lead time):**
  - Live payment-provider account and the acquirer conversation (V6).
  - DPAs and data-flow map (V2).
  - V1 spike.
  - Finance controller named as approver.
  - Inventory of what consumes billing output today.
  - Baseline of the current manual cycle: hours per close, error and correction rate.

Exit check (V0): see §6.

## 5. Phases

- **Phase 1 — Tenancy, money and billing core**
  - Unlocks: correct invoices for flat and seat plans, generated deterministically.
  - Depends on: V0; V1 passed (R3); rounding rule and tenant count in hand.
  - Tasks:
    1. Tenancy keys, RLS and tenant context in the request and in jobs. V3 runs first on this slice, then becomes a regression check in CI.
    2. Money type and double-entry ledger schema. UPDATE and DELETE are revoked at the database level.
    3. Versioned catalog and prices.
    4. Subscription lifecycle: create, change with proration, cancel.
    5. Idempotency framework.
    6. Deterministic invoice generation from (price version, ledger, period).
    7. Audit log of every change, with the actor.
  - Rollback: per-release image rollback. Data only exists for the internal tenant, so a schema rework still costs nothing customer-facing.
  - Exit check: V3 passes; the invariant part of V4 passes; a synthetic bill run at 10× tenant count finishes within the bill-run budget.

- **Phase 2 — Usage metering and rating** (can run in parallel with Phase 3; both precede Phase 4)
  - Unlocks: usage-priced invoice lines.
  - Depends on: Phase 1 catalog and meter contract; peak rate supplied.
  - Tasks:
    1. Event schema in a registry. Fields are limited to tenant, meter, quantity, timestamp and `event_id`; anything else is rejected.
    2. Authenticated, rate-limited ingestion API per tenant.
    3. Kafka, then the raw immutable store.
    4. Dedupe and the late-event window.
    5. Aggregation per (tenant, meter, period).
    6. Rating against the pinned price version.
    7. Period close and freeze.
    8. Re-rating from raw data.
  - Rollback: re-rate from the raw store. Meters can be switched off on invoices per flag.
  - Exit check: V5 passes; storage and stream cost at projected volume is recorded against the budget.

- **Phase 3 — Payments, tax, dunning, reconciliation**
  - Unlocks: collecting money and issuing tax-correct invoices.
  - Depends on: Phase 1; V0.
  - Tasks:
    1. Payment-provider customer and payment method via hosted fields, using restricted API keys.
    2. Payment intents with idempotency keys.
    3. Webhook receiver: signature check, dedupe, out-of-order handling.
    4. Daily reconciliation of ledger, invoices and provider balance transactions, alerting on any unexplained difference.
    5. Tax call at finalization. An invoice is never finalized without tax; if the tax engine is down, finalization waits.
    6. Credit notes, refunds (second approver above a threshold), and disputes coming in.
    7. Dunning that sends reminders only.
  - Exposure: the internal tenant and staff cards only.
  - Rollback: flag off. Money already collected goes back by refund, never by editing records.
  - Exit check: V4 passes in full; V6 passes; V7 passes for each jurisdiction going live.

- **Phase 4 — Shadow billing for cohort 1**
  - Unlocks: evidence that the system matches reality.
  - Depends on: Phases 1–3; Phase 2 only for usage-priced tenants; V2.
  - Tasks:
    1. Versioned, idempotent import of cohort 1 contracts (10–20 tenants covering every plan type) from CRM or spreadsheets.
    2. Bill runs in shadow: email sender and charging hard-disabled by flag and allow-list.
    3. Parity against the manual invoices for 2 cycles.
  - Rollback: discard the shadow data and re-run.
  - Exit check: V8 passes.

- **Phase 5 — Live waves, tenant portal, GL export, entitlements**
  - Unlocks: the system becomes the system of record per tenant.
  - Depends on: V3, V4, V6, V7, V8; dunning and suspension policy before any suspension is enforced.
  - Tasks:
    1. Wave 1 (5 friendly card-paying tenants), then 10%, then 50%, then all. A wave widens only after its cycle shows clean reconciliation, a payment failure rate within the baseline, no billing Sev1/Sev2 incident, and billing tickets within the baseline from Phase 0.
    2. Tenant portal behind product SSO with a tenant-admin role: invoices view, payment method update via hosted fields.
    3. Idempotent journal export to the GL.
    4. Entitlements read API with a cache.
  - Rollback: a per-tenant flag sends the tenant back to manual billing from the next cycle. A wrong invoice already issued is corrected with a credit note, using the runbook.
  - Exit check: all waves pass their check; the entitlement p99 budget is met; billing API SLOs are set once the availability input exists.

- **Phase 6 — Decommission manual billing; DR**
  - Unlocks: one system of record.
  - Depends on: Phase 5 complete; V9.
  - Tasks:
    1. Archive the legacy records with checksums.
    2. Revoke legacy access.
    3. DR drill: restore the ledger from PITR within the RTO, and rebuild payment state from provider events.
  - Point of no return: deprovisioning the legacy process and data, guarded by V9.
  - Exit check: V9 passes; the DR drill meets the RPO/RTO.

## 9. Methodology exceptions
None. The skeleton's live $1 charge is the company's own money, refunded, with card entry hosted by the provider. That follows the normal method, so it is not an exception.

## 10. Deliberately deferred
- **Revenue recognition (ASC 606/IFRS 15 schedules).** Pulled forward when finance closes from the GL export, or when an audit requires it.
- **Billing as a service** (Connect onboarding, tenant-configurable catalogs, public API and webhooks). Pulled forward if missing input 1 resolves that way; then re-plan from Step 1.
- **Regional cells for data residency.** Pulled forward by a residency obligation found in V2 or a contract; the `home_region` seam already exists.
- **Real-time prepaid credit drawdown.** Pulled forward if the price book needs it; this flips the consistency row.
- **Multi-currency and multiple issuing entities beyond the first.** Pulled forward when sales commits to a second currency or entity.
- **Self-serve plan changes in the portal.** Pulled forward once V8 covers proration cases and Phase 5 wave 2 is clean.
- **Marketplace billing (AWS or Azure Marketplace).** Pulled forward by the first signed marketplace deal.
- **Ledger partitioning or sharding.** Pulled forward when bill-run writes pass 1,000/s or a run misses the 6 h budget.
- **Service suspension for non-payment.** Pulled forward once product and legal approve the policy; until then dunning only sends reminders.
