Classification\n- **What:** Move a live, on-premises patient scheduling system to the cloud. That means its database, application tier and all its connections to other systems (EHR, admissions feed, patient portal, reminders, billing, reports). Clinics must be able to book appointments the whole time.\n- **Type:** Mostly infrastructure, plus software where systems connect to each other. **Not AI.**\n- **Dominant constraint:** **Correctness, then compliance (HIPAA).** Availability is a hard requirement, but it must never come at the cost of a booking that's wrong.\n- **Worst failure:** **Split-brain.** Both the old and new databases accept bookings at the same moment, so a time slot gets double-booked or an appointment is silently lost. Patients turn up to slots that don't exist, or nobody expects them. The close second is protected patient data (PHI) getting out through a misconfigured cloud resource or log.\n- **Defining \"without downtime\" honestly:** No unplanned outage, and staff never lose the ability to *view* schedules. At the one moment the database writer switches over, **new bookings pause for up to 60 seconds.** During that pause the routing layer holds them and retries. A true zero-pause switch would need both databases accepting writes at once, which is the split-brain risk above, so this plan rejects it.\n- **Assumptions to confirm in week 1. These can change the plan:**\n  1. Does the vendor support cloud hosting, and does it offer a SaaS version?\n  2. Client design: are the front-end apps web/API-based, or thick clients that connect straight to the database?\n  3. Which database engine, and which version?\n  4. Is a BAA (HIPAA Business Associate Agreement) already signed with a cloud provider?\n  5. What recovery targets (RTO/RPO) are agreed with clinical leadership?\n\n## 2. Clinician workstation\n  2. Hospital network\n  3. **New routing layer**, installed on-prem in pure pass-through, with an instant bypass\n  4. Cloud managed database, fed by **CDC from the production on-prem database**, for the scheduling tables only\n  7. The two responses are compared, field by field\n- **Real response:** Clinicians still get the on-prem answer.
