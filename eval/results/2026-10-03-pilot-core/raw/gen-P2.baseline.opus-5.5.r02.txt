Key flows\n\n### 4.1 Subscription lifecycle (state machine)\n\n```\n incomplete ──pay──► trialing ──trial_end──► active ◄──────── paid ──┐\n     │                   │                     │                      │\n  expire              cancel              payment_failed          past_due ──dunning exhausted──► unpaid/canceled\n     ▼                   ▼                     ▼                      ▲\n incomplete_expired   canceled             past_due ──────────────────┘\n                                   active ⇄ paused (collection paused)\n```\n\n- Transitions happen only through a single `SubscriptionStateMachine` that enforces guards and writes `subscription_event` history rows.\n- Every transition emits an event, and the Entitlements service consumes it to grant or revoke access. Example: on `past_due`, keep access during a grace period. On `unpaid`, restrict access.\n\n### 4.2 Plan changes and proration\n\nChanges are written as **time-bounded `subscription_item` rows**. They never overwrite existing rows. For an upgrade at time *t*:\n\n1. Close the old item with `ends_at = t`, and open a new item with `starts_at = t`.\n2. Proration engine: credit `old_amount × unused_fraction`, charge `new_amount × remaining_fraction`. Compute the fraction by **seconds**, and document that choice (some businesses prefer days).\n3. The tenant-level policy decides whether you invoice now (`always_invoice`), roll the charge into the next invoice (`create_prorations`), or skip proration (`none`).\n4. Downgrades usually take effect at period end, which avoids issuing refunds.\n\nProperty-based tests should check that any sequence of changes within a period sums to the same result as a direct calculation.\n\n### 4.3 Usage metering pipeline\n\n```\nClient SDK/API ──► Ingest API (stateless, per-tenant rate limit, schema validation)\n  event = {idempotency_key, tenant_id, account_id, meter, value, timestamp, properties}\n      ──► Kafka topic usage.raw (partitioned by tenant_id:account_id)\n      ──► Dedup stage (keyed state store, idempotency window ≥ billing period + grace)\n      ──► ClickHouse: raw events (ReplacingMergeTree) + materialized hourly rollups\n```\n\n- **Late events.** Accept events until `period_end + grace` (for example, 1 hour to 3 days). Usage that arrives later goes onto the next invoice as an `adjustment` line tied to the original period. It never reopens a finalized invoice.\n- **Corrections.** Use compensating events (negative values or explicit `void` events). Don't delete events.\n- **Real-time visibility.** The same rollups feed \"current spend\" dashboards, spend alerts and usage limits in the Entitlements service, such as hard caps for prepaid plans.\n- **Noisy neighbours.** Each tenant gets ingest quotas and its own Kafka partition key, so one heavy tenant only saturates its own partitions. Very large tenants get dedicated topics or cells.\n\n### 4.4 The billing run\n\n```\nScheduler (every minute):\n  SELECT subscriptions WHERE next_bill_at <= now() AND shard = me\n  FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED  →  enqueue BillSubscription(sub_id, period_start)\n\nWorker: BillSubscription  (idempotency_key = sub_id + period_start)\n  1. That makes it deterministic and replayable, and lets you diff invoices between versions (see §8).\n\n### 4.5 Payments, dunning and reconciliation\n\n- **Provider abstraction:** a `PaymentGateway` interface with Stripe and Adyen adapters, plus offline methods (wire, ACH, invoice/PO). Routing can depend on currency or region.\n- **PCI scope:** card data never touches your servers. Use hosted fields or Elements, store **tokens only**, and aim for SAQ-A.\n- **Payment saga:** `pending → processing → succeeded | failed | requires_action (3DS/SCA)`. Inbound webhooks are verified, stored raw, deduplicated by `provider_event_id`, and processed asynchronously.\n- **Dunning:** configurable per tenant, e.g., retries on days 1, 3, 5 and 7 with smart retry timing. Key flows\n\n### 4.1 Subscription lifecycle (state machine)\n\n```\n incomplete ──pay──► trialing ──trial_end──► active ◄──────── paid ──┐\n     │                   │                     │                      │\n  expire              cancel              payment_failed          past_due ──dunning exhausted──► unpaid/canceled\n     ▼                   ▼                     ▼                      ▲\n incomplete_expired   canceled             past_due ──────────────────┘\n                                   active ⇄ paused (collection paused)\n```\n\n- Transitions happen only through a single `SubscriptionStateMachine` that enforces guards and writes `subscription_event` history rows.\n- Every transition emits an event, and the Entitlements service consumes it to grant or revoke access. Example: on `past_due`, keep access during a grace period. On `unpaid`, restrict access.\n\n### 4.2 Plan changes and proration\n\nChanges are written as **time-bounded `subscription_item` rows**. They never overwrite existing rows. For an upgrade at time *t*:\n\n1. Close the old item with `ends_at = t`, and open a new item with `starts_at = t`.\n2. Proration engine: credit `old_amount × unused_fraction`, charge `new_amount × remaining_fraction`. Compute the fraction by **seconds**, and document that choice (some businesses prefer days).\n3. The tenant-level policy decides whether you invoice now (`always_invoice`), roll the charge into the next invoice (`create_prorations`), or skip proration (`none`).\n4. Downgrades usually take effect at period end, which avoids issuing refunds.\n\nProperty-based tests should check that any sequence of changes within a period sums to the same result as a direct calculation.\n\n### 4.3 Usage metering pipeline\n\n```\nClient SDK/API ──► Ingest API (stateless, per-tenant rate limit, schema validation)\n  event = {idempotency_key, tenant_id, account_id, meter, value, timestamp, properties}\n      ──► Kafka topic usage.raw (partitioned by tenant_id:account_id)\n      ──► Dedup stage (keyed state store, idempotency window ≥ billing period + grace)\n      ──► ClickHouse: raw events (ReplacingMergeTree) + materialized hourly rollups\n```\n\n- **Late events.** Accept events until `period_end + grace` (for example, 1 hour to 3 days). Usage that arrives later goes onto the next invoice as an `adjustment` line tied to the original period. It never reopens a finalized invoice.\n- **Corrections.** Use compensating events (negative values or explicit `void` events). Don't delete events.\n- **Real-time visibility.** The same rollups feed \"current spend\" dashboards, spend alerts and usage limits in the Entitlements service, such as hard caps for prepaid plans.\n- **Noisy neighbours.** Each tenant gets ingest quotas and its own Kafka partition key, so one heavy tenant only saturates its own partitions. Very large tenants get dedicated topics or cells.\n\n### 4.4 The billing run\n\n```\nScheduler (every minute):\n  SELECT subscriptions WHERE next_bill_at <= now() AND shard = me\n  FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED  →  enqueue BillSubscription(sub_id, period_start)\n\nWorker: BillSubscription  (idempotency_key = sub_id + period_start)\n  1. Load items effective in period (versioned prices)\n  2. Rate recurring lines (in advance) + usage lines (in arrears, from ClickHouse)\n  3. Apply discounts/coupons → apply prepaid credits/commitments (drawdown)\n  4. Create DRAFT invoice  (editable window, e.g., 1h, for usage grace / manual review)\n  5. Finalize: call Tax service → assign gapless invoice number → status=open\n  6. Post ledger txn:  DR Accounts Receivable / CR Revenue (or Deferred Revenue) / CR Tax Liability\n  7. Emit invoice.finalized → Payments attempts collection (if auto-charge)\n  8. Advance subscription period (same DB txn as 5–6)\n```\n\n- **First-of-month spikes.** Spread billing anchors (each account's anchor is its signup date), scale workers horizontally per shard, and treat the pipeline as a queue, not a cron job that runs everything at once.\n- **Rating is a pure function**: `(items, price_versions, usage_aggregates, credits) → lines`. That makes it deterministic and replayable, and lets you diff invoices between versions (see §8).\n\n### 4.5 Payments, dunning and reconciliation\n\n- **Provider abstraction:** a `PaymentGateway` interface with Stripe and Adyen adapters, plus offline methods (wire, ACH, invoice/PO). Routing can depend on currency or region.\n- **PCI scope:** card data never touches your servers. Use hosted fields or Elements, store **tokens only**, and aim for SAQ-A.\n- **Payment saga:** `pending → processing → succeeded | failed | requires_action (3DS/SCA)`. Inbound webhooks are verified, stored raw, deduplicated by `provider_event_id`, and processed asynchronously.\n- **Dunning:** configurable per tenant, e.g., retries on days 1, 3, 5 and 7 with smart retry timing. Each retry sends an email asking the customer to update their card. Failure codes decide what's retryable (`insufficient_funds` is worth retrying; `stolen_card` is not). When retries run out, the subscription moves to `unpaid` or `canceled`, and the receivable is optionally written off with a ledger entry.\n- **Reconciliation:** a daily job matches payments in the ledger against provider settlement reports and payouts, net of fees. Any mismatch raises an alert and goes to an ops queue.\n\n---\n\n## 5. Multi-tenancy design\n\n| Concern | Approach |\n|---|---|\n| **Data isolation** | `tenant_id` is on every row and leads every PK and index. Postgres **Row-Level Security** uses `SET app.tenant_id` per connection or transaction, as a second guard behind app-level scoping. |\n| **Scaling** | Shard by `tenant_id` (Citus distributed tables, or a tenant→shard directory). Each tenant's data stays on one shard, so financial transactions stay local. |\n| **Isolation tiers** | *Pooled* (the default), or a *dedicated cell* for large or regulated tenants: its own DB and workers, same code. A tenant directory routes requests. Tenants can be moved between cells with logical replication. |\n| **Data residency** | Region-pinned cells (EU/US). The tenant directory is global; financial data stays in its region. |\n| **Noisy neighbours** | Per-tenant rate limits (ingest and API), per-tenant fair queueing in billing workers, and query timeouts. |\n| **Per-tenant config** | Invoice numbering prefix and sequence, dunning policy, proration policy, tax settings, branding, net terms, and grace periods. |\n| **Encryption** | Encryption at rest plus envelope encryption of PII using per-tenant DEKs in KMS, which lets you crypto-shred a tenant's data on offboarding. |\n| **Billing-as-a-service variant** | Add a `merchant` layer above `tenant`. Each merchant brings its own payment-provider credentials (Stripe Connect or similar), legal entity, tax registrations and invoice templates. Isolation, webhooks and API keys then become fully tenant-facing products. |\n\n---\n\n## 6. Cross-cutting concerns\n\n- **Tax:** an adapter over Stripe Tax, Avalara or Anrok. Store the tax quote on the invoice so it can be replayed. Handle VAT reverse-charge with a validated VAT ID, US sales-tax nexus, and tax-exempt certificates.\n- **Multi-currency:** each billing account has one currency, and each price version is defined per currency. Avoid live FX in pricing. If you must convert, store the rate and its source on the invoice. Ledgers are kept per currency.\n- **Revenue recognition (ASC 606 / IFRS 15):** finalizing an invoice posts to deferred revenue, and a nightly job recognizes revenue ratably across the service period. Export journal entries to the ERP (NetSuite, etc.).\n- **Credits, commitments and prepaid balances:** these are ledger accounts, not columns. Drawdown order is: promotional credits, then prepaid, then commitment, then overage. Commit contracts include a true-up at the end of the term.\n- **Invoices and documents:** PDFs are rendered asynchronously from finalized invoices, stored in object storage with a content hash, and never regenerated with different numbers.\n- **Outbound webhooks:** signed (HMAC), retried with backoff, ordered per account, and replayable from a delivery log.\n- **Audit:** an immutable audit log of every manual action (who issued a credit, who voided what), and four-eyes approval above a set amount.\n\n---\n\n## 7. Reliability and consistency\n\n- **Consistency model:** strong (ACID) for invoices, payments, the ledger and subscriptions. Eventual for entitlements, analytics and notifications.\n- **Idempotency:** client `Idempotency-Key` headers are stored with response hashes for 24–72 hours. Jobs use deterministic keys. Unique constraints on `provider_ref` and `invoice.idempotency_key` are the last line of defence.\n- **Concurrency:** optimistic locking (`version`) on subscriptions, `SKIP LOCKED` job claiming, and a per-account serialization lock during finalization.\n- **Failure handling:** if tax fails, the invoice stays in draft and is retried. If payment fails, dunning takes over. If ClickHouse is unavailable, billing for usage subscriptions is delayed (with an alert), never billed as zero.\n- **SLOs:** entitlement check p99 under 10 ms; usage ingest 99.99% availability and p99 under 100 ms; invoices finalized within 2 hours of period end.\n\n---\n\n## 8. Testing strategy (where billing systems usually fail)\n\n1. **Test clocks:** advance a sandbox account through a year of renewals, trials, upgrades and dunning in seconds.\n2. **Property-based tests** for proration, tiered pricing and credit drawdown. Check invariants such as ledger balance, invoice total equals the sum of lines, and no negative amounts due.\n3. **Golden invoices:** a corpus of real scenarios that must produce byte-identical line items.\n4. **Shadow billing:** before a migration or pricing-engine change, run the new engine alongside the old one and diff every invoice. Ship only when the diff is empty or explained.\n5. **Chaos tests:** duplicate webhooks, out-of-order events, and worker crashes between finalizing an invoice and posting the ledger entry.\n\n---\n\n## 9.
